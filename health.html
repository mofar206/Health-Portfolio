The product approach

Useful information needs
clear boundaries.

This project explores how people might compare access information. Its strongest contribution is the interface and the questions it makes visible. The neighborhood records remain illustrative, so the project does not treat them as evidence for local funding or care decisions.

The design decision

Separate three tasks: checking a neighborhood, interpreting service capacity, and comparing food-access scenarios. Keep the same navigation across the explorations so users can move between related questions.

The evidence decision

Show published county indicators separately from neighborhood scenarios. Include source context and report unavailable data honestly instead of silently replacing it with a sample value.

The next research step

Ask potential users which information helps them decide what to do next. Replace scenario records with documented, geographically aligned data before evaluating whether the tool improves information-finding tasks.

A note on the data

Neighborhood records are illustrative scenarios from the original project. They are not verified estimates or a directory of currently available care. Public CDC county indicators are shown separately with their observation years and source information.
